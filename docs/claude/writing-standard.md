# Writing standard (Simplified Technical English)

Plainline writes instructions in the style of ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE), the plain-language rule set from aircraft maintenance manuals. Plain words fit what Plainline sells: finished jobs that an owner understands at a glance.

## Where it applies
| Text | How strict |
|---|---|
| SOPs, the delivery guide, the Fact Sheet, hand-off steps, setup steps, the owner dashboard's help text | Full checklist below |
| Monthly reports, welcome doc, thank-you note, feedback request, package menu | Checklist items 1 to 5 |
| Website copy (`index.html`, client sites) | Items 2, 3 and 5 only. Copy keeps the business's own voice |
| Customer text replies from an assistant | Not applied. They follow the assistant's tone and the 280-character rule |
| The client agreement and other legal text | Not applied. A lawyer sets that wording |

## Checklist
1. Put the key point first. One topic for each paragraph, 6 sentences or fewer.
2. Use the active voice. Write "Ben sends the invoice", not "The invoice is sent".
3. Use plain, common words. No jargon, idioms or slang. No vague verbs such as "handle" or "deal with". Say the exact action.
4. Use one word for one thing. If a step says "hand-off", every step says "hand-off". Do not change to "escalation".
5. Keep sentences short: 20 words or fewer for an instruction, 25 or fewer for a description.
6. Write one instruction in each sentence. Start it with the verb: "Open the calendar."
7. Put a warning or caution before the step it applies to, in its own sentence.
8. Use a numbered list for steps in order. Use bullets for items that are not in order.
